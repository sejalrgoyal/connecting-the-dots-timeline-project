# Gender and Slavery in Colonial America

This project is an interactive timeline exploring the relationship between gender and slavery in Colonial America. It highlights key historical events that shaped how slavery developed and how gender influenced the status and lives of enslaved people.

## Project Overview
The timeline focuses on several important historical moments:

- **1656 – Elizabeth Key Wins Her Freedom**  
  Elizabeth Key successfully sued for her freedom and that of her son in Virginia, arguing that her father was a free Englishman and that she had been baptized as a Christian.

- **1662 – Virginia Slave Act**  
  The Virginia Assembly passed a law stating that a child's status (free or enslaved) would follow the condition of the mother. This law helped establish hereditary slavery in the colonies.

- **1773 – Birth of Ona Judge**  
  Ona Judge was born into slavery at Mount Vernon and later became known for escaping from the household of George and Martha Washington in 1796.

Together, these events demonstrate how legal systems and social structures shaped slavery and reinforced gender roles in colonial America.

## Purpose
This website was created as a digital history project for **HIST 2384**.  
The goal is to present historical developments in a clear and interactive format that helps viewers understand how gender and law influenced the evolution of slavery.
